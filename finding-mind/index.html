A book in three parts

Finding Mind

How the origin of Mind recapitulates the origin of Life

Lucius Gregory Meredith

Three rings, two thresholds. The leading is not a frame around the picture. It is the picture.

There are two events in the geologic record that have no explanation. The first is the transition from an Earth without life to an Earth with life. The second is the transition from an Earth without mind to an Earth with mind.

This book argues they are the same event.

Not a miracle, and not an accident. A fixed point. In the space of all known models of computation there are places the system cannot help but arrive at, and life and mind are two of them. The mechanism is reflection: the moment a process can quote itself, become its own location, and read itself back.

@P  a process, quoted, becomes a name
*x  a name, dereferenced, becomes a process again

What follows from that is not a metaphor. In a reflective calculus the rule that steps a computation forward is already the rule that recombines genetic material. Two forms meet at a name, each reads what the other carries, and both continue changed. Crossover, without anyone arranging it.

There is no fitness function in the sky. A better word than fit is responsible: what persists is whatever can keep showing up and answering. And because bisimulation is the finest possible classification of program behaviour, it is also the ceiling on what any machine can ever learn. Everything an artificial mind can know about another mind, it knows through that lattice.

Which is the argument for building the picture this way. A window is assembled from pieces that cannot be subdivided further, held in a lattice of lead, and it does nothing at all until light passes through it.
